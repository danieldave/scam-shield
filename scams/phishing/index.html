Phishing Scams: how to spot them and what to do

Quick answer: A phishing scam is a fake SMS, email or chat message that pretends to come from your bank, school or an app, so you click a link or share your password, PIN or OTP. Real banks in Nigeria never ask for your PIN or OTP by message or phone call.

Warning signs of phishing scams

  • Says your account will be blocked or closed
  • Asks for your PIN, password or OTP code
  • Link looks almost right, but not quite

How this scam works, step by step

  1. The scammer copies the name and style of a bank or company you know.
  2. The message creates panic: your account will be blocked or closed.
  3. A link takes you to a fake page that looks real.
  4. Anything you type there (password, PIN, OTP) goes straight to the scammer.

What to do if you get one

  1. Stop. Don't click. Do not click links, open attachments or reply. Scammers want you to act fast.
  2. Don't share. Never share your password, PIN, OTP, BVN or photos of your cards.
  3. Check & verify. Contact the person or organisation using a number or app YOU already trust, not the one in the message.
  4. Report it. Tell a trusted adult. Use the 'Report' button on the app or platform, and report to the real organisation.
  5. Keep devices safe. Use strong passwords, turn on two-step verification and keep your apps updated.

What to do if you already clicked, replied or paid

Close the page and do not type anything else. If you typed a password, change it now from the real app and turn on two-step verification. If it was your bank PIN or OTP, tell a trusted adult and call your bank on the number on the back of the card.

Questions people ask

What is a phishing scam?

A phishing scam is a fake SMS, email or chat message that pretends to come from your bank, school or an app, so you click a link or share your password, PIN or OTP. Real banks in Nigeria never ask for your PIN or OTP by message or phone call.

What should I do if I fell for a phishing scam?

Close the page and do not type anything else. If you typed a password, change it now from the real app and turn on two-step verification. If it was your bank PIN or OTP, tell a trusted adult and call your bank on the number on the back of the card.

Will my bank ever ask for my OTP?

No. Your OTP is a one-time code that proves it is really you. A bank, POS agent or 'customer care' worker never needs it. Anyone who asks for it is trying to steal from you.

How can I tell if a link is fake?

Look closely at the address. Fake links add extra words like '-secure-login' or use strange endings. The safest habit is not to click at all: open your bank app or type the real address yourself.

What should I do with a phishing message?

Do not reply or click. Take a screenshot, report and block the sender in the app, tell a trusted adult, then delete it.

Other scams to know

  • Fake Prizes & GiveawaysPromises of rewards, gifts or money that are too good to be true.
  • Romance ScamsFake friends or partners online who build trust, then ask for money.
  • Online Shopping ScamsFake sellers, items that do not exist, or payment tricks.
  • Tech Support ScamsFake 'helpers' who want to control your phone or computer.
  • Investment ScamsFalse promises of big returns with 'no risk'.
  • Fake Job OffersJobs that ask YOU to pay before you start.
  • Impersonation ScamsSomeone pretends to be a friend, family member or official.
