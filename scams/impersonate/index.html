Impersonation Scams: how to spot them and what to do

Quick answer: An impersonation scam is when someone pretends to be a friend, family member, teacher or official, often from a new number, and asks for urgent money or secrets. The fix is simple: contact the real person using a number you already have.

Warning signs of impersonation scams

  • New number, 'it's me'
  • An emergency and a request for money
  • Asks you to keep it secret

How this scam works, step by step

  1. A message says 'Hi, it's me, I changed my number'.
  2. There is an emergency and they need money now.
  3. They ask you to keep it secret, often 'don't tell Mum'.
  4. Some pretend to be an exam office, police or customer care to scare you.

What to do if you get one

  1. Stop. Don't click. Do not click links, open attachments or reply. Scammers want you to act fast.
  2. Don't share. Never share your password, PIN, OTP, BVN or photos of your cards.
  3. Check & verify. Contact the person or organisation using a number or app YOU already trust, not the one in the message.
  4. Report it. Tell a trusted adult. Use the 'Report' button on the app or platform, and report to the real organisation.
  5. Keep devices safe. Use strong passwords, turn on two-step verification and keep your apps updated.

What to do if you already clicked, replied or paid

Do not send anything. Call the real person on their old, saved number. Tell a trusted adult. If you already sent money, contact your bank through official channels quickly and keep the messages.

Questions people ask

What is an impersonation scam?

An impersonation scam is when someone pretends to be a friend, family member, teacher or official, often from a new number, and asks for urgent money or secrets. The fix is simple: contact the real person using a number you already have.

What should I do if I fell for an impersonation scam?

Do not send anything. Call the real person on their old, saved number. Tell a trusted adult. If you already sent money, contact your bank through official channels quickly and keep the messages.

My friend messaged from a new number asking for money. What do I do?

Call their old number or ask a question only the real friend knows. Real friends will understand that you checked.

Why do scammers say 'don't tell your parents'?

Because a trusted adult would spot the trick. Any message that asks you to keep secrets from your family is a red flag.

Can an exam office ask me to pay to release my result?

Be very careful. Exam bodies publish fees on their official websites. A random message asking you to pay a number today is a scam.

Other scams to know

  • Phishing ScamsFake messages that trick you into sharing passwords, PINs or codes.
  • Fake Prizes & GiveawaysPromises of rewards, gifts or money that are too good to be true.
  • Romance ScamsFake friends or partners online who build trust, then ask for money.
  • Online Shopping ScamsFake sellers, items that do not exist, or payment tricks.
  • Tech Support ScamsFake 'helpers' who want to control your phone or computer.
  • Investment ScamsFalse promises of big returns with 'no risk'.
  • Fake Job OffersJobs that ask YOU to pay before you start.
