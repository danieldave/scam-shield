Online Shopping Scams: how to spot them and what to do

Quick answer: An online shopping scam is a fake seller who offers items at a very low price, asks you to pay into a personal account first, and then never delivers. Real sellers usually allow pay-on-delivery, have reviews and give a real address.

Warning signs of online shopping scams

  • Price far too low
  • Pay first into a personal account
  • No pay-on-delivery, no address, no reviews

How this scam works, step by step

  1. An advert shows a phone, shoes or clothes for a tiny price.
  2. The seller says 'last few pieces' to rush you.
  3. They refuse pay-on-delivery and want a transfer first.
  4. After payment, they block you or ask for a 'delivery fee'.

What to do if you get one

  1. Stop. Don't click. Do not click links, open attachments or reply. Scammers want you to act fast.
  2. Don't share. Never share your password, PIN, OTP, BVN or photos of your cards.
  3. Check & verify. Contact the person or organisation using a number or app YOU already trust, not the one in the message.
  4. Report it. Tell a trusted adult. Use the 'Report' button on the app or platform, and report to the real organisation.
  5. Keep devices safe. Use strong passwords, turn on two-step verification and keep your apps updated.

What to do if you already clicked, replied or paid

Keep the chat, the advert and the payment receipt. Report the seller on the app, tell a trusted adult and contact your bank through official channels to ask about the transfer.

Questions people ask

What is an online shopping scam?

An online shopping scam is a fake seller who offers items at a very low price, asks you to pay into a personal account first, and then never delivers. Real sellers usually allow pay-on-delivery, have reviews and give a real address.

What should I do if I fell for an online shopping scam?

Keep the chat, the advert and the payment receipt. Report the seller on the app, tell a trusted adult and contact your bank through official channels to ask about the transfer.

How do I know if an online seller is real?

Check for reviews from real buyers, a physical address, pay-on-delivery and a business account name that matches the shop. A very low price is the biggest warning sign.

Is it safe to pay into a personal account?

It is risky. Scammers prefer personal accounts because they are hard to trace. Use pay-on-delivery or a trusted platform's payment system.

What is a fake payment alert?

It is a fake SMS or screenshot that looks like a bank credit alert. Sellers should always confirm money in their real bank app before giving out goods.

Other scams to know

  • Phishing ScamsFake messages that trick you into sharing passwords, PINs or codes.
  • Fake Prizes & GiveawaysPromises of rewards, gifts or money that are too good to be true.
  • Romance ScamsFake friends or partners online who build trust, then ask for money.
  • Tech Support ScamsFake 'helpers' who want to control your phone or computer.
  • Investment ScamsFalse promises of big returns with 'no risk'.
  • Fake Job OffersJobs that ask YOU to pay before you start.
  • Impersonation ScamsSomeone pretends to be a friend, family member or official.
