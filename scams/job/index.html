Fake Job Offers: how to spot them and what to do

Quick answer: A fake job offer promises big pay for easy work, skips proper interviews, and asks you to pay a 'registration', 'training' or 'processing' fee before you start. A real employer pays you; they never ask you to pay them to get the job.

Warning signs of fake job offers scams

  • Big pay for very easy work
  • Registration or training fee
  • Interview only on chat

How this scam works, step by step

  1. An advert offers big daily pay for simple work from home.
  2. No experience or interview is needed.
  3. You must pay a fee or send your BVN or NIN to 'register'.
  4. After paying, the job never comes, or they ask for more fees.

What to do if you get one

  1. Stop. Don't click. Do not click links, open attachments or reply. Scammers want you to act fast.
  2. Don't share. Never share your password, PIN, OTP, BVN or photos of your cards.
  3. Check & verify. Contact the person or organisation using a number or app YOU already trust, not the one in the message.
  4. Report it. Tell a trusted adult. Use the 'Report' button on the app or platform, and report to the real organisation.
  5. Keep devices safe. Use strong passwords, turn on two-step verification and keep your apps updated.

What to do if you already clicked, replied or paid

Do not pay more. If you sent your BVN, NIN or bank details, tell a trusted adult and contact your bank. Report the advert and warn friends who saw it.

Questions people ask

What is a fake job offer scam?

A fake job offer promises big pay for easy work, skips proper interviews, and asks you to pay a 'registration', 'training' or 'processing' fee before you start. A real employer pays you; they never ask you to pay them to get the job.

What should I do if I fell for a fake job offer scam?

Do not pay more. If you sent your BVN, NIN or bank details, tell a trusted adult and contact your bank. Report the advert and warn friends who saw it.

Do real jobs ask for a registration fee?

No. Real employers pay you. A fee before you start is the clearest sign of a fake job.

Are scholarship offers that ask for a fee real?

Be very careful. Real scholarships are announced on official school or government websites, and you apply there. A message asking for a 'processing fee' to secure a slot is a scam.

Should I send my BVN or NIN to get a job?

Never send ID numbers to someone you only met on chat. They can be used to open accounts or steal money in your name.

Other scams to know

  • Fake Prizes & GiveawaysPromises of rewards, gifts or money that are too good to be true.
  • Romance ScamsFake friends or partners online who build trust, then ask for money.
  • Online Shopping ScamsFake sellers, items that do not exist, or payment tricks.
  • Tech Support ScamsFake 'helpers' who want to control your phone or computer.
  • Investment ScamsFalse promises of big returns with 'no risk'.
  • Impersonation ScamsSomeone pretends to be a friend, family member or official.
