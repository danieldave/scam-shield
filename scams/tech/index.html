Tech Support Scams: how to spot them and what to do

Quick answer: A tech support scam is a scary pop-up, call or message that says your phone or computer has a virus or an expired licence, then asks you to call a number, install an app or pay to 'fix' it. Real companies do not contact you this way.

Warning signs of tech support scams

  • Scary pop-up: 'virus detected!'
  • Asks you to install an app so they can control your device
  • Asks for payment to 'fix' it

How this scam works, step by step

  1. A loud pop-up says 'VIRUS DETECTED!' or 'Your files will be deleted'.
  2. It gives a number to call or an app to install.
  3. The 'agent' asks you to install a remote-control app like a screen-sharing tool.
  4. Then they ask for payment or steal what is on your device.

What to do if you get one

  1. Stop. Don't click. Do not click links, open attachments or reply. Scammers want you to act fast.
  2. Don't share. Never share your password, PIN, OTP, BVN or photos of your cards.
  3. Check & verify. Contact the person or organisation using a number or app YOU already trust, not the one in the message.
  4. Report it. Tell a trusted adult. Use the 'Report' button on the app or platform, and report to the real organisation.
  5. Keep devices safe. Use strong passwords, turn on two-step verification and keep your apps updated.

What to do if you already clicked, replied or paid

Close the pop-up or turn off the device. If you installed an app, uninstall it and change your passwords from another device. Tell a trusted adult. If you paid, contact your bank quickly.

Questions people ask

What is a tech support scam?

A tech support scam is a scary pop-up, call or message that says your phone or computer has a virus or an expired licence, then asks you to call a number, install an app or pay to 'fix' it. Real companies do not contact you this way.

What should I do if I fell for a tech support scam?

Close the pop-up or turn off the device. If you installed an app, uninstall it and change your passwords from another device. Tell a trusted adult. If you paid, contact your bank quickly.

Can a website know my phone has a virus?

No. A web page cannot scan your phone. Any page that says it found viruses is lying to scare you.

Should I install an app a stranger tells me to?

Never. Apps that control your screen let the stranger see and do everything, including opening your bank app.

How do I close a scary pop-up?

Close the browser tab or the whole browser. Do not tap buttons inside the pop-up, not even 'Cancel'.

Other scams to know

  • Phishing ScamsFake messages that trick you into sharing passwords, PINs or codes.
  • Fake Prizes & GiveawaysPromises of rewards, gifts or money that are too good to be true.
  • Romance ScamsFake friends or partners online who build trust, then ask for money.
  • Online Shopping ScamsFake sellers, items that do not exist, or payment tricks.
  • Investment ScamsFalse promises of big returns with 'no risk'.
  • Fake Job OffersJobs that ask YOU to pay before you start.
  • Impersonation ScamsSomeone pretends to be a friend, family member or official.
