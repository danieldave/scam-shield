Is the Federal Government grant message on WhatsApp real?

Quick answer: No. Forwarded messages that say the Federal Government is "giving out" a cash grant to everyone, with a link to apply, are scams. Real government programmes are announced on official websites that end in .gov.ng and never ask you to share a message with your contacts.

What the message looks like (made-up example)

Made-up exampleFG ₦50,000 Cash Grant Ongoing! No BVN required. Apply now: fg-grant-portal.example. Share with 15 friends to qualify.

The warning signs

  • It tells you to share with friends or groups before you "qualify".
  • The link does not end in .gov.ng.
  • It promises free money to everybody, with no checks.

What our offline checker sees in this message

  • Rushes you with a deadline or a threat (words: ongoing, now)
  • Free money from 'government' or a big company (words: cash grant, grant)
  • Contains a link to click (words: .example)

Paste any message you receive into the checker to see its warning signs in seconds.

How the real thing works

Real government support is announced by ministries and agencies on .gov.ng websites and in trusted news. It never spreads by "share with 15 contacts".

What to do if you get this message

  1. Stop. Don't click. Do not click links, open attachments or reply. Scammers want you to act fast.
  2. Don't share. Never share your password, PIN, OTP, BVN or photos of your cards.
  3. Check & verify. Contact the person or organisation using a number or app YOU already trust, not the one in the message.
  4. Report it. Tell a trusted adult. Use the 'Report' button on the app or platform, and report to the real organisation.
  5. Keep devices safe. Use strong passwords, turn on two-step verification and keep your apps updated.

If you already replied, clicked or paid

Do not pay more. If you sent your BVN, NIN or bank details, tell a trusted adult and contact your bank. Report the advert and warn friends who saw it.
