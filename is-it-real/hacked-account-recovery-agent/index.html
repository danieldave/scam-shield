Can a "hacker" on social media recover my account for a fee?

Quick answer: No. People who advertise account recovery in comments and DMs are scammers who take your money and sometimes the account too. Recovery is done only through the app's own help page.

What the message looks like (made-up example)

Made-up exampleI recovered my account thanks to this expert! Message him now, he is fast and trusted.

The warning signs

  • Recommendations in comments from strangers.
  • Payment first.
  • They ask for your email password or a code.

What our offline checker sees in this message

  • Rushes you with a deadline or a threat (words: now)
  • 'It's me', new number, or pretends to be an official (words: this expert)

Paste any message you receive into the checker to see its warning signs in seconds.

How the real thing works

Use the "forgot password" or "hacked account" help inside the real app.

What to do if you get this message

  1. Stop. Don't click. Do not click links, open attachments or reply. Scammers want you to act fast.
  2. Don't share. Never share your password, PIN, OTP, BVN or photos of your cards.
  3. Check & verify. Contact the person or organisation using a number or app YOU already trust, not the one in the message.
  4. Report it. Tell a trusted adult. Use the 'Report' button on the app or platform, and report to the real organisation.
  5. Keep devices safe. Use strong passwords, turn on two-step verification and keep your apps updated.

If you already replied, clicked or paid

Close the pop-up or turn off the device. If you installed an app, uninstall it and change your passwords from another device. Tell a trusted adult. If you paid, contact your bank quickly.

This is a type of tech support scam.
