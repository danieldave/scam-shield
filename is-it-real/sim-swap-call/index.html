Someone called claiming to be from my network and asked for a code. Is it real?

Quick answer: Treat it as a scam. Callers who ask for a code, your NIN or details "to upgrade your SIM" may be trying a SIM swap, which moves your phone number to their SIM so they receive your bank codes.

What the message looks like (made-up example)

Made-up exampleThis is your network provider. We are upgrading your line to 5G. Kindly confirm the code sent to you now.

The warning signs

  • They called you.
  • They want a code or your personal details.
  • Your line later loses signal for no reason.

What our offline checker sees in this message

  • Asks for PIN, password, OTP or codes (words: code sent)
  • Rushes you with a deadline or a threat (words: now)
  • 'It's me', new number, or pretends to be an official (words: network provider)

Paste any message you receive into the checker to see its warning signs in seconds.

How the real thing works

If your line suddenly stops working, tell an adult and contact your network and bank quickly through official channels.

What to do if you get this message

  1. Stop. Don't click. Do not click links, open attachments or reply. Scammers want you to act fast.
  2. Don't share. Never share your password, PIN, OTP, BVN or photos of your cards.
  3. Check & verify. Contact the person or organisation using a number or app YOU already trust, not the one in the message.
  4. Report it. Tell a trusted adult. Use the 'Report' button on the app or platform, and report to the real organisation.
  5. Keep devices safe. Use strong passwords, turn on two-step verification and keep your apps updated.

If you already replied, clicked or paid

Close the page and do not type anything else. If you typed a password, change it now from the real app and turn on two-step verification. If it was your bank PIN or OTP, tell a trusted adult and call your bank on the number on the back of the card.

This is a type of phishing scam.
