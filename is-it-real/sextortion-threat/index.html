Someone is threatening to share my private pictures. What do I do?

Quick answer: Do not pay and do not send more. This is called sextortion and it is a crime against you; it is not your fault. Stop replying, keep the evidence, block and report the account, and tell a trusted adult today.

What the message looks like (made-up example)

Made-up exampleI have your pictures. Pay ₦50,000 in 2 hours or I send them to all your followers and your school.

The warning signs

  • A stranger moved the chat to private very fast.
  • They asked for pictures or a video call.
  • Now they threaten and set a deadline.

What our offline checker sees in this message

  • Asks you to pay or send money (words: pay, pay ₦)
  • Rushes you with a deadline or a threat (words: hours)

Paste any message you receive into the checker to see its warning signs in seconds.

How the real thing works

Paying almost never stops it; they ask again. Apps have tools to report this and to stop images spreading, and a trusted adult can help you use them.

What to do if you get this message

  1. Stop. Don't click. Do not click links, open attachments or reply. Scammers want you to act fast.
  2. Don't share. Never share your password, PIN, OTP, BVN or photos of your cards.
  3. Check & verify. Contact the person or organisation using a number or app YOU already trust, not the one in the message.
  4. Report it. Tell a trusted adult. Use the 'Report' button on the app or platform, and report to the real organisation.
  5. Keep devices safe. Use strong passwords, turn on two-step verification and keep your apps updated.

If you already replied, clicked or paid

Stop sending money or photos. Do not be ashamed: these scammers are very skilled. Save the chat, block and report the account, and tell a trusted adult. If they threaten you, do not pay; tell an adult straight away.
