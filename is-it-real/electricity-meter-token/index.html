Is the message offering cheap prepaid meter tokens or a free meter real?

Quick answer: Treat it as a scam. Offers of half-price electricity tokens, "meter bypass" or a free meter for a registration fee from private numbers are tricks to take money or account details.

What the message looks like (made-up example)

Made-up exampleGet your prepaid meter free! Government scheme. Pay ₦6,000 registration to our officer and it is installed tomorrow.

The warning signs

  • A private number, not your electricity company.
  • A registration fee.
  • Promises of tomorrow.

What our offline checker sees in this message

  • Asks you to pay or send money (words: pay, registration, pay ₦)
  • Prize, win or something free (words: free)

Paste any message you receive into the checker to see its warning signs in seconds.

How the real thing works

Electricity matters are handled by your distribution company's official office and channels.

What to do if you get this message

  1. Stop. Don't click. Do not click links, open attachments or reply. Scammers want you to act fast.
  2. Don't share. Never share your password, PIN, OTP, BVN or photos of your cards.
  3. Check & verify. Contact the person or organisation using a number or app YOU already trust, not the one in the message.
  4. Report it. Tell a trusted adult. Use the 'Report' button on the app or platform, and report to the real organisation.
  5. Keep devices safe. Use strong passwords, turn on two-step verification and keep your apps updated.

If you already replied, clicked or paid

Keep the chat, the advert and the payment receipt. Report the seller on the app, tell a trusted adult and contact your bank through official channels to ask about the transfer.

This is a type of online shopping scam.
