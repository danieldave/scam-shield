Is the message saying I have admission and should pay an acceptance fee real?

Quick answer: Check before paying. Scammers send fake admission messages and collect "acceptance fees" into personal accounts. Real admission status is seen on the official admission portal, and fees are paid through the school's official channel.

What the message looks like (made-up example)

Made-up exampleCongratulations, you have been offered admission. Pay ₦25,000 acceptance fee to the admission officer today to keep your space.

The warning signs

  • Payment to a personal account.
  • Deadline of today.
  • It did not come through the official portal.

What our offline checker sees in this message

  • Asks you to pay or send money (words: pay, fee, pay ₦)
  • Rushes you with a deadline or a threat (words: today)
  • Prize, win or something free (words: congratulations)

Paste any message you receive into the checker to see its warning signs in seconds.

How the real thing works

Log in to the official portal yourself, with a parent or teacher beside you.

What to do if you get this message

  1. Stop. Don't click. Do not click links, open attachments or reply. Scammers want you to act fast.
  2. Don't share. Never share your password, PIN, OTP, BVN or photos of your cards.
  3. Check & verify. Contact the person or organisation using a number or app YOU already trust, not the one in the message.
  4. Report it. Tell a trusted adult. Use the 'Report' button on the app or platform, and report to the real organisation.
  5. Keep devices safe. Use strong passwords, turn on two-step verification and keep your apps updated.

If you already replied, clicked or paid

Do not send anything. Call the real person on their old, saved number. Tell a trusted adult. If you already sent money, contact your bank through official channels quickly and keep the messages.

This is a type of impersonation scam.
